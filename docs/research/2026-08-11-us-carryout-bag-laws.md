## California — SB 1053 detail

**What changed, effective 1 January 2026:**

1. **Thick "reusable" plastic film bags are eliminated.** SB 270's entire certification regime for
   reusable plastic film grocery bags — former PRC §§42281, 42281.5, 42282, 42282.1 — was repealed as of
   1/1/2026. CalRecycle's own page now reads: "The reusable grocery bag requirements enacted by SB 270
   are no longer in effect, due to the passage of SB 1053." The 2.25-mil "reusable" loophole is closed.
   ([leginfo SB 1053](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB1053),
   [CalRecycle](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/plastics/carryoutbags/))
2. **Paper-only at checkout.** Under PRC §42283 a store "shall not provide, distribute, or sell a carryout
   bag at the point of sale" except a **recycled paper bag**, or specified compostable / non-handled bags.
3. **Recycled content.** PRC §42280(e): the bag must be curbside-recyclable in most CA households, must
   print manufacturer name, country of manufacture and PCR %, and — **on and after January 1, 2028 —
   contain a minimum of 50% postconsumer recycled material**. In the 2026–2027 window the pre-existing
   standard applies: **40% PCR, or 20% PCR for bags with capacity of 8 lb or less.**
4. **The 10-cent minimum.** §42283 requires "**not less than ten cents ($0.10)**" per recycled paper bag.
   Money "**shall be retained by the store** and may be used only" for compliance costs, bag costs, or an
   educational reusable-bag campaign. **Nothing is remitted to the state.** WIC/EBT customers get bags free.
5. **Penalties.** PRC §42285(a): **$1,000/day** first violation, **$2,000/day** second, **$5,000/day**
   third and subsequent. Enforceable by a city, county, or the state (AG).
6. **Restaurants/QSR: out of scope.** "Store" under PRC §42280(f) is limited to (1) full-line self-service
   retailers ≥$2M gross sales selling dry groceries/canned goods/perishables, (2) retail ≥10,000 sq ft with
   a licensed pharmacy, (3)–(4) convenience/foodmart with an ABC Type 20 or 21 license, or (5) a voluntary
   opt-in. **A restaurant or QSR is not a "store."** A QSR co-located inside a covered grocery/convenience
   store is a different question, as is a QSR holding a Type 20/21 off-sale license.

**Exemptions from "carryout bag":** pharmacy prescription bags, non-handled protective bags preventing
cross-contamination, precheckout bags (PRC §42281.2), garment/dry-cleaning bags, and compostable bags
where the jurisdiction permits curbside composting.

## The QSR-relevant split, summarised

- **Restaurants fully covered:** NJ, WA, OR, VT, RI, ME (ban only)
- **Restaurants expressly exempt:** CA, NY, DE
- **Restaurants mostly exempt in practice:** CO (excused from the ban if they serve individual portions
  for immediate consumption and aren't a grocery/convenience store; never subject to the fee)
- **Ambiguous:** CT (definition points at the sales-tax "retailer," no carve-out either way)
- **Only a QSR *fee* obligation, and only sometimes:** OR (5¢ but only on reusable *plastic* bags — paper
  can be free), WA (8¢ paper / 12¢ plastic), VT (10¢ paper)
- **Fee is remitted, not kept:** CO (60% to local govt), NY (40% to municipality, rest to EPF), and
  historically CT (to DRS, 2019–2021). Everywhere else the business keeps it.

> **Why this matters to the data model.** Several of these fees are collected *from the customer* and
> partly **remitted to government**. That is neither an EPR fee nor a tax on the business — it is a
> pass-through with a remittance obligation and its own filing. See `ObligationClass =
> 'pass_through_charge'` in `docs/EXPOSURE_CALCULATOR_SPEC.md` §5.

## Known gaps

- **HI** county ordinance specifics (charge amount, restaurant coverage, thickness) — could not reach a
  primary source; do not rely on the 15¢ figure without checking ROH Ch. 9 Art. 9.
- **DE** penalty amounts.
- **ME** — the statute text still reads Apr 22, 2020 / Jan 15, 2021; the COVID postponement to July 1,
  2021 is real but the delaying act's number was not verified.
- **RI** effective date — not stated in the codified chapter; check §2 of P.L. 2022 ch. 176.
- **WA** — one Ecology page described an *additional 4¢* surcharge tied to 4-mil bags for 2026–2027 that
  does not appear in the RCW text. The RCW is authoritative: 12¢ plastic / 8¢ paper from 1/1/2026, 4-mil
  requirement from 1/1/2028. The surcharge claim is **unverified**.
- **Local preemption matters a lot for QSR:** WA preempts local bag ordinances enacted after 4/1/2020; VT
  occupies the whole field from 7/1/2020; RI supersedes all state and local law in the field; OR has a
  preemption section (ORS 459A.759). **CA, NY, CO, CT and NJ leave more room for city ordinances that
  *do* reach restaurants even where the state law doesn't** — a QSR chain cannot rely on the state
  exemption alone in those five.
